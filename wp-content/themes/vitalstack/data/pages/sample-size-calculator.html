Two questions, one tool

Before a survey: "How many people should I ask?" Choose your confidence level and the margin of error you can accept. For 95% confidence and ±5%, you need 385 people, whether you survey a city or a whole country.

After a survey: "How sure am I?" Enter how many people you asked and how many said yes. For example, 312 out of 500 is 62.4%, with a 95% confidence interval of about 58.2% to 66.6%.

Why 385 people is enough for a whole country

The margin of error depends on the number of people you ask, not on the size of the population. Once the population is much bigger than your sample, it hardly matters whether it is a lakh or a crore. If your population is small (say a college of 2,000 students), enter it in the optional box: the calculator applies the finite population correction and tells you a smaller number.

The formulas

  • Sample size for a proportion: n = z² × p(1 − p) ÷ e², with p = 0.5 (the safest guess) and z = 1.96 for 95%.
  • Confidence interval: p ± z × √(p(1 − p) ÷ n).

To halve the margin of error you need about four times as many people. That is why polls rarely go beyond 1,000 to 2,000 people.

Good samples matter more than big ones

A random sample of 400 beats a biased sample of 40,000. Avoid surveying only your friends, only people who choose to reply, or only one city when you want an answer about India.
