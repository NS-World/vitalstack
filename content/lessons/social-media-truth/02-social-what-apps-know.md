Apps learn far more from **how** you use them than from what you post: what you pause on, what you skip, when you're online, who you talk to, where you are. Put together, this builds a detailed picture of your interests, habits and even your mood. That picture powers targeted ads and recommendations.

## What gets collected

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| What you give | Name, phone, email, birthday, photos, posts, contacts (if allowed) |
| What you do | Likes, follows, searches, watch time, what you skip, when you're active |
| From your device | Device model, IP address, approximate or precise location, other apps (sometimes) |
| From elsewhere | Websites and apps that share data with ad platforms through tracking tools |
| What they infer | Interests, likely age, relationship status, income level, mood patterns |

The inferred part surprises most people: you never typed "I'm planning a wedding", but your behaviour can say it loudly.

## How targeted ads work (simply)

Advertisers don't usually receive your name. They tell the platform "show this to people aged 22–30 in Pune interested in fitness", and the platform's data picks the people. So the platform holds the detailed profile, and that's what makes it valuable.

## Data breaches

Large platforms and smaller apps alike have suffered leaks of phone numbers, emails and profile data. Leaked data fuels scams: a scammer who knows your name and phone number sounds much more convincing. That's another reason to share less and use unique passwords.

## Settings worth changing

- **Download your data** (most apps offer "Download your information") to see what they hold.
- **Ad preferences:** review and remove interest categories; limit ads based on activity from other sites where offered.
- **Location:** allow "only while using the app" or turn it off; review location history.
- **Contacts sync:** turn it off if you don't need it.
- **Phone settings:** on iPhone, ask apps not to track; on Android, review the advertising ID settings.
- **Delete old apps and accounts** you no longer use.

## Is my phone listening to me?

Researchers generally haven't found apps secretly recording conversations for ads, and it would be expensive and easy to detect. The simpler truth is that your searches, browsing, location, friends' interests and watch history are usually enough to predict what you want. Still, review **microphone permissions** and allow them only for apps that clearly need them.

::tip Spend 10 minutes this week in the privacy settings of the one app you use most. Turning off contact sync, precise location and off-app activity removes a big part of your data trail.

## Summary

- Apps collect what you share, how you behave, device data and data from other sites.
- They infer interests and life events to target ads.
- Leaked data makes scams more convincing; share less, use unique passwords.
- Download your data and tighten ad, location and contact settings.
