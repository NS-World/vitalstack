Social media has turned anyone into a broadcaster, including scammers and people paid to sell you things. Learning to separate genuine advice from advertising and fraud is one of the most valuable skills you can have online.

💬 | The group | "Join my free WhatsApp group for sure-shot tips. 3% a day guaranteed!" | dev
💸 | The trap | Small profits appear in an app, then withdrawals are blocked unless you pay "tax". | chip

## Common social media money traps

| Trap | Red flag |
|---|---|
| Fake giveaways | "Pay a small delivery fee to claim your free iPhone" |
| Investment and trading groups | Guaranteed returns, "limited seats", pressure to invest more |
| Fake celebrity endorsements | AI-edited videos of famous people promoting apps or schemes |
| Task and part-time jobs | "Earn by liking posts", then pay to unlock bigger tasks |
| Fake shopping pages | New pages with huge discounts and UPI-only payment |
| Account "verification" | DMs saying your account will be deleted unless you "verify" via a link |

## Paid promotion and disclosure

Influencers often earn money for promoting products. That's legal, but in India the advertising standards body (ASCI) and consumer rules expect paid promotions to be **clearly disclosed** (like "#ad", "Paid partnership"). If a glowing review hides that it was paid, treat it as an advertisement, not advice.

## Fake followers and fake proof

Followers, likes and comments can be bought. Profit screenshots can be edited in seconds. Luxury cars and offices can be rented for a shoot. Big numbers prove only that someone is good at getting attention.

## How to judge advice

Ask:

1. **How do they earn money?** Courses, referrals, paid promotions, trading commissions?
2. **Are claims specific and checkable?** Or just "life-changing", "secret", "guaranteed"?
3. **Do they show losses and mistakes,** or only wins?
4. **Are they registered** where it matters? In India, people giving investment advice for a fee are expected to be registered with SEBI; you can check on SEBI's website.
5. **Is there pressure?** Limited time, limited seats and "don't miss out" are sales tactics.

## Protecting yourself

- Never pay a "fee" to receive a prize.
- Don't move money to apps or accounts suggested by strangers in groups.
- Check reviews outside the platform before buying from a new page.
- Report fake pages and scam ads using the platform's report button.

::tip If something online promises to make you rich quickly, assume the person making money quickly is the one promoting it.

## Summary

- Fake giveaways, trading groups, AI-faked endorsements and task jobs are common traps.
- Paid promotions should be disclosed; undisclosed ones are ads pretending to be advice.
- Followers and screenshots prove little; check how people earn and whether they're registered.
- Never pay to receive, never chase guaranteed returns, and report scam pages.
