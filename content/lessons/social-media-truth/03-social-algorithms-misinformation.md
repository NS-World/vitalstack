Your feed is not a neutral window on the world. A **recommendation algorithm** picks, from millions of posts, the few it predicts will keep you engaged. Engaging and **true** are not the same thing. Shocking, angry and emotional content often spreads fastest, and that has real effects on what people believe.

## How the algorithm decides

It looks at signals like: what you watched to the end, what you liked, commented on or shared, what similar people engaged with, and what's trending. Then it shows more of whatever keeps you on the app. It doesn't check whether something is true or good for you.

## Why outrage spreads

Posts that trigger strong emotions (anger, fear, shock) get more comments and shares, so the algorithm shows them to more people. That creates a loop: creators learn that dramatic content gets reach, and calm, accurate content struggles to compete.

## Echo chambers

If you engage only with one viewpoint, your feed slowly fills with more of the same, and different views disappear. Everyone around you seems to agree, which makes beliefs feel more certain than the evidence supports.

## Deepfakes and AI content

AI can now make realistic fake videos, voices and images: fake celebrity endorsements of investment schemes, fake news clips, fake voice notes from "relatives". Seeing is no longer believing.

## The 5-step check before you share

1. **Pause.** Strong emotion is a reason to slow down.
2. **Source:** who made this originally? A known news outlet, an official account, or "forwarded many times"?
3. **Search:** look for the claim on reliable news sites or fact-checkers. Reverse image search photos and video frames.
4. **Date and place:** old or foreign content is often reshared as "new" and "local".
5. **Ask:** "Would I bet money that this is true?" If not, don't forward it.

## Break your bubble (a little)

- Follow a few reliable sources with different viewpoints.
- Use "Not interested" on content that makes you angry but teaches you nothing.
- Prefer people and sources who correct their mistakes openly.

::tip When a family member forwards something false, reply privately and kindly with the real source. People rarely change their minds when corrected in public.

## Summary

- Algorithms show what keeps you engaged, not what's true.
- Outrage spreads fast; echo chambers make beliefs feel more certain than they are.
- Deepfakes mean images and videos need checking too.
- Pause, check the source, search, check date and place, then decide.
