Social media isn't simply "good" or "bad". It connects friends, helps people learn and gives communities a voice. But many people notice they feel worse after scrolling: more anxious, more envious, more tired. Researchers continue to study the effects, especially for teenagers, and the evidence suggests **how** you use it matters a lot.

## The highlight reel problem

People post their best moments, filtered and edited. You see everyone's **highlights** but live your own **behind-the-scenes**. Comparing the two makes ordinary life feel like failure, even when you're doing fine.

## FOMO (fear of missing out)

Seeing friends at events or trips without you can trigger the feeling that everyone else is living better. Constant checking to "not miss anything" can increase anxiety rather than reduce it.

## Likes and self-worth

When likes and views become a scoreboard for your value, every post feels like a test. Numbers go up and down for reasons that have nothing to do with you: algorithms, timing, trends.

## Cyberbullying

Online harassment can follow you everywhere, at any hour. If it happens: don't reply in anger, keep screenshots, block and report the accounts, tell someone you trust, and for serious threats report on **cybercrime.gov.in** or call **1930**.

## Sleep

Scrolling late at night delays sleep, and bright screens and exciting content keep the brain alert. Poor sleep affects mood, memory and studies more than most people realise.

## Healthier habits

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| Passive scrolling | Messaging or calling a friend directly |
| Following accounts that make you feel small | Following creators who teach, inspire or make you laugh |
| Checking the moment you wake up | 30 phone-free minutes after waking |
| Phone in bed | Charging it outside the bedroom |
| Posting for likes | Posting for yourself and close friends, with likes hidden |

Many apps let you **hide like counts**, **mute** accounts without unfollowing, and **limit** sensitive content. Use them.

## When to get help

If you feel persistently low, anxious or unable to stop even when you want to, talk to someone you trust. In India you can reach the government's **Tele-MANAS** mental health helpline at **14416** (free, 24×7). Asking for help is a sign of strength.

::tip Try a "48-hour detox" from one app this weekend. Notice how you feel on Monday. Even short breaks help many people reset their habits.

## Summary

- Comparing your normal life with others' highlights distorts how you see yourself.
- FOMO, like-counting and late-night scrolling can affect mood and sleep.
- Curate your feed, hide likes, and keep the phone out of the bedroom.
- If you feel persistently low, talk to someone; Tele-MANAS 14416 is free and 24×7.
