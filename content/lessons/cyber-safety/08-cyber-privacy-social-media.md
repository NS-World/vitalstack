Every post tells the world something about you: where you live, where you are right now, who your family is, when you're on holiday. Most of the time that's harmless. But scammers and stalkers collect these pieces to make convincing scams. Privacy isn't about hiding; it's about choosing who sees what.

::scene The holiday post
✈️ | Posting live | "Off to Goa for a week! 🏖️" with the family photo and a check-in. | dev
🕵️ | Someone watching | A stranger now knows the house is empty, and the pet's name used in passwords. | bugsy
⚙️ | Settings | Zoya switches to "friends only" and posts holiday photos after returning. | chip
😌 | Peace of mind | Same memories shared, much less risk. | byte
::end

## What scammers can learn from your profile

- **Your answers to security questions:** pet names, school, birthday, mother's name.
- **When your home is empty:** live travel posts and check-ins.
- **Your family and friends:** useful for "your son is in trouble" calls and impersonation.
- **Your workplace and role:** used for fake HR mails and boss-impersonation scams.

## Privacy settings to change today

| Setting | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Profile visibility | Private, or friends only |
| Who can find you by phone number or email | Friends, or nobody |
| Phone number and birth year on profile | Hidden |
| Location tags on posts | Off by default; add only when you choose |
| Who can add you to groups (WhatsApp) | "My contacts" (Settings → Privacy → Groups) |
| Last seen, profile photo, about | "My contacts" |
| Tag approval | On: review tags before they appear |

## Photos and location

Photos can contain **location data** (GPS) in the file. Most social apps remove it, but files sent as documents may keep it. Post travel photos **after** you're back, and avoid showing your house number, vehicle number, school uniforms and ID cards.

## Fake profiles and romance scams

Be careful with friendly strangers who move fast: compliments, a sad story, then a need for money, a "crypto opportunity", or a private video call. Check by reverse image search on their photos, and never send money or intimate photos to someone you haven't met in real life.

## Deepfakes and sextortion

AI can now create fake videos and voices. Criminals use this for fake celebrity endorsements, fake "relative in trouble" voice calls, and blackmail.

- If someone threatens to share intimate or fake images: **don't pay**, don't delete the evidence, block the account, and report on **cybercrime.gov.in** (there's a category for women and children) or call **1930**.
- Use a family code word to verify urgent voice calls.

## Children and teenagers

- Use parental controls and child accounts where available.
- Keep devices in shared spaces for younger children.
- Talk openly: tell them they can always come to you, even if they made a mistake. Shame keeps children silent, and silence helps criminals.

::tip Once a year, search your own name and phone number online and check what a stranger can see. Then tighten what you don't like.

## Summary

- Scammers build convincing stories from small details you post.
- Set profiles to private, hide phone numbers and turn off location tags.
- Post travel photos after returning; watch for fast-moving strangers.
- For blackmail or deepfake threats: don't pay, keep evidence, report on 1930.
