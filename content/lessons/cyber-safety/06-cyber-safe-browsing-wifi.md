The browser is your window to the internet, and some windows open onto fake shops, fake government portals and booby-trapped downloads. A few habits make everyday browsing much safer, at home or on free airport Wi-Fi.

## What the padlock really means

The padlock (HTTPS) means the connection between you and the website is **encrypted**: nobody in between can read it. It does **not** mean the website is honest. Scam sites get padlocks for free too. Always check **who** you are talking to (the domain), not just **how** (the padlock).

## Spotting fake websites

| Check | Real sites | Fake sites |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | The brand's known domain | Extra words, odd endings (.shop, .online, .xyz), misspellings |
| Prices | Normal sale prices | Unbelievable discounts on everything |
| Contact | Address, support, return policy | Only a WhatsApp number or form |
| Payment | Cards, UPI, cash on delivery | "UPI only", "pay to this personal account" |
| Age | Years old, many reviews | Created weeks ago, copied reviews |

For government services, use the official **.gov.in** sites and typing the address yourself. Search ads can lead to lookalike portals that charge "fees" for free services.

## Safe downloads

- Download apps and software only from **official stores or the maker's website**.
- Be very careful with files named like `invoice.pdf.exe` or `.apk` files sent on WhatsApp: they can install malware.
- Cracked or "free premium" apps and games are a favourite way to spread malware.

## Browser extensions

Extensions can read everything you do in your browser. Keep only those you really use, from known developers, and remove the rest. An extension that suddenly asks for new permissions after an update deserves suspicion.

## Public Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi at cafes, stations and airports is convenient but shared with strangers.

- **Prefer your mobile data** for banking and payments.
- Check the **exact network name** with staff; attackers create lookalikes such as "Airport_Free_WiFi".
- Make sure sites show **HTTPS**, and don't ignore browser certificate warnings.
- Turn off **auto-connect** to open networks, and forget the network when you leave.
- A trusted VPN adds protection on public Wi-Fi, but avoid unknown free VPN apps that may sell your data.

::tip Bookmark the sites you use for money: your bank, your UPI app's site, government portals. Opening a bookmark is safer than searching every time, because scam ads often appear at the top of search results.

## Summary

- The padlock means "encrypted", not "trustworthy": always read the domain.
- Fake shops show unreal prices, odd domains, no policies and UPI-only payments.
- Download only from official sources; prune browser extensions.
- On public Wi-Fi, prefer mobile data for money and avoid lookalike networks.
