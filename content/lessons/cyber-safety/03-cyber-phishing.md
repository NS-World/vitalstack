**Phishing** is when someone pretends to be a trusted company or person to make you click a link, open a file or share information. The name comes from "fishing": they throw out bait and wait for a bite. It arrives by email, SMS ("smishing"), WhatsApp, social media and even QR codes.

## The 7 red flags

1. **Urgency or threat:** "within 24 hours", "account suspended", "legal action".
2. **Too good to be true:** lottery wins, free iPhones, huge refunds, easy money jobs.
3. **A link you didn't expect,** especially shortened links (bit.ly, tinyurl) or odd domains.
4. **Asks for secrets:** OTP, PIN, password, card number, Aadhaar or PAN photos.
5. **Generic greeting:** "Dear customer" instead of your name.
6. **Small mistakes:** spelling errors, odd logos, strange sender addresses like `support@sbi-kyc-update.online`.
7. **Unusual request from a known person:** your "boss" asks for gift cards, a "friend" needs money urgently on a new number.

One red flag is enough to stop and verify.

## How to read a link before you tap it

On a computer, hover over the link to see where it really goes. On a phone, press and hold the link to preview it. Then read the **domain**: the part just before the first single `/`.

| Link | Real domain | Safe? |
|---|---|---|
| `https://www.onlinesbi.sbi/login` | onlinesbi.sbi | ✅ The bank's real domain |
| `https://onlinesbi.sbi.kyc-update.in/login` | kyc-update.in | ❌ The real domain is at the end |
| `https://amaz0n-offers.shop` | amaz0n-offers.shop | ❌ Zero instead of "o", odd ending |
| `http://192.168.4.20/paytm` | an IP address | ❌ Real companies use names |

The trick in the second row is common: scammers put a famous name at the **start** of their own domain. Always read from the right side of the domain.

## Common phishing stories in India

- **KYC update:** "Your account will be blocked, update PAN/KYC here."
- **Electricity bill:** "Power will be cut tonight, call this officer."
- **Courier/customs:** "Parcel on hold", "illegal items found in your parcel".
- **Income tax refund:** "Claim your refund now" with a fake portal.
- **Job offers:** "Earn ₹5,000 a day liking videos" (task scams).
- **Fake shopping sites** with huge discounts that take payment and never deliver.

## If you already clicked

1. **Don't enter anything** on the page. Close it.
2. If you entered a password, **change it immediately** from the real app or website, and turn on 2FA.
3. If you entered card or bank details, **call your bank** and block the card.
4. If you lost money, call **1930** (India's cyber crime helpline) right away and report at **cybercrime.gov.in**.
5. Report fraud SMS and calls on the government's **Sancharsaathi** portal (Chakshu).

::tip Make it a habit to open apps and websites yourself instead of using links in messages. If your bank or courier really needs something, you'll see it when you log in normally.

## Summary

- Phishing uses fake messages to make you click, pay or share secrets.
- Watch for urgency, rewards, odd links, requests for secrets and small mistakes.
- Read a link's real domain from the right; famous names at the start mean nothing.
- Already clicked? Change passwords, call your bank, and report on 1930.
