Most people imagine a hacker in a hoodie typing green code to break into a bank. Real life is much simpler and much closer to home: a phone call from a "bank officer", a WhatsApp message about a parcel, a link to "update your KYC". Most cyber crime today doesn't break computers. It **tricks people**. That's why cyber safety is a life skill for everyone, not just for IT people.

## How most attacks actually work

Almost every scam follows the same recipe:

| Step | What the scammer does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | Gets your attention | "Your electricity will be cut tonight" |
| 2. Pressure | Creates fear, greed or urgency | "Only 10 minutes left", "You won ₹25 lakh" |
| 3. Trust | Pretends to be someone official | Bank, police, courier, your boss, a relative |
| 4. Ask | Gets the one thing they need | An OTP, a UPI PIN, a payment, a screen-sharing app, a click |

If you notice the **pressure** and the **ask**, you can stop almost any scam, no matter how clever the story is.

## Who gets targeted?

Everyone. Students get fake job and internship offers. Working people get fake HR mails and "task" jobs. Parents get calls about a child "in trouble". Older people get KYC and pension scams. Small shops get fake payment screenshots. Scammers don't need to know you personally: they send thousands of messages and wait for one person to reply.

## The five habits that stop most attacks

1. **Never share OTPs, PINs or passwords.** No bank, company or government office will ever ask for them.
2. **Pause before you click or pay.** Urgency is the scammer's best weapon. A real problem will still be there in 10 minutes.
3. **Verify using a number you already trust.** Call the number on the back of your card or on the official website, not the one in the message.
4. **Turn on two-step verification** for email, WhatsApp, banking and social media.
5. **Keep your phone and apps updated,** and install apps only from the official app store.

## Summary

- Most cyber crime tricks people instead of breaking computers.
- Scams follow a pattern: hook, pressure, fake trust, and an ask.
- Never share OTPs or PINs, pause before acting, and verify with a trusted number.
- Two-step verification and updates block most account takeovers.
