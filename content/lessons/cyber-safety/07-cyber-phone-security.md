Your phone receives your OTPs, runs your UPI and banking apps, and stores your photos, chats and documents. For most people it is the single most important device to protect. The steps below take about 15 minutes and protect you for years.

## Lock it properly

- Use a **6-digit PIN or longer** plus fingerprint or face unlock. Avoid patterns: people can see them over your shoulder.
- Set the screen to lock after 30 seconds to 1 minute.
- **Hide OTP and message previews** on the lock screen (notification settings), so a stranger can't read them.

## Apps and permissions

- Install apps only from the **Play Store or App Store**. Never install `.apk` files sent on WhatsApp, Telegram or SMS.
- Before installing, check the developer name, downloads and reviews. Fake apps copy famous names and logos.
- Review permissions regularly (Settings → Privacy / Permission manager). Be suspicious of apps that ask for:
  - **SMS access** (can read OTPs),
  - **Accessibility** (can see and tap things on your screen),
  - **Display over other apps** and **device admin**.
- Remove apps you no longer use.

## Updates

Updates fix security holes that attackers already know about. Turn on **automatic updates** for your phone and apps, and restart when asked. An old phone that no longer gets security updates should not be your main banking phone.

## SIM swap

In a **SIM swap**, a criminal gets a new SIM for your number (using fake documents or a lie to the shop) and receives your OTPs. Warning signs: your phone suddenly shows **"No service"** for a long time while others around you have signal.

- Act fast: contact your mobile operator and bank immediately if this happens.
- Use **authenticator apps or passkeys** instead of SMS codes where possible.
- Check which mobile numbers are registered on your ID using the government's **Sancharsaathi** portal, and report ones you don't recognise.

## If your phone is lost or stolen

1. Use **Find My Device** (Android) or **Find My** (iPhone) to lock or erase it remotely.
2. Call your mobile operator to block the SIM.
3. Inform your bank and block UPI from another device or by calling the bank.
4. Report the device on Sancharsaathi (CEIR) so it can be blocked.
5. Change your email password from another device.

::tip Set this up today, before you need it: Find My Device turned on, a recovery email and phone number on your accounts, and automatic photo backup. Recovery is ten times easier when it's prepared in advance.

## Summary

- Use a strong PIN plus biometrics and hide OTP previews on the lock screen.
- Install only from official stores; never install APKs from messages; review permissions.
- Keep the phone updated; watch for sudden "No service" (possible SIM swap).
- Prepare Find My Device and backups before your phone is ever lost.
